Evaluating Data Sources for the Corcoran Clay Confining Unit

Research Assistant · Department of Geophysics, Stanford Doerr School of Sustainability

June 2026 – September 2026

Advisors: Prof. Rosemary J. Knight & Ph.D. Candidate Jonathan Mells, GEM Center

Background

The Corcoran Clay is a regionally extensive confining layer beneath California's San Joaquin Valley that separates shallow and deep groundwater systems. Understanding where it thins, breaks, or varies in thickness matters directly for managing groundwater recharge and extraction across the Central Valley — one of the most heavily used aquifer systems in the country.

What I worked on

I georeferenced and spatially digitized historical USGS geologic plates in ArcGIS Pro, converting older raster maps into control-point-registered vector layers aligned with modern Corcoran Clay datasets. From there, I built a spatial well-log database by geoprocessing USGS subsurface texture records into queryable GIS point feature classes, which made it possible to query and visualize subsurface composition across many well locations at once.

The core of the project was a comparative data-evaluation framework: assessing airborne electromagnetic (AEM) survey data against legacy well-log records to identify which data sources give the most reliable picture of the confining unit in a given area — work that feeds into broader research on sustainable groundwater management in California.
